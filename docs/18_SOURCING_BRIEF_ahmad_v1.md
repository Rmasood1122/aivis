## 2. THE ONE RULE THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE REST

**Never edit the messages in §6. Not one word. Copy and paste them exactly.**

The wording is filtered against a banned list. Ten specific words — *evidence, proof, method,
methodology, verify, accurate, trust, reproducible, data quality, confidence* — cannot appear
anywhere in the outreach, and cannot be spoken by Rehan in the first five questions of the
call.

The reason is not style. The whole study measures whether these people bring those ideas up
**on their own, without being led there.** If the invitation mentions trust or proof, then
anything they say about trust or proof afterwards is an echo of us, not a finding. One word
in one message and that conversation is scientifically worthless — it still costs an hour and
it produces nothing.

A version of this message was drafted elsewhere containing the phrase *"what people actually
trust"* and it had to be thrown away. That is how easy this is to break.

**If a message feels too short or too plain: that is deliberate. Send it as written.**

## 3. WHO TO FIND — TWO SEGMENTS, BOTH REQUIRED

We need **40 names total**, split across two groups. Both groups are required; a list of only
Group A is not usable.

### GROUP A — 25 names · agencies selling AI visibility with no instrument

Marketing, PR, or SEO agency people whose company advertises something like *AI Search
Optimization, AI visibility, GEO, AEO, LLM SEO, ChatGPT optimization, answer engine
optimization.*

**Titles:** Founder · Co-Founder · Owner · Managing Director · CEO · Head of SEO ·
Head of Digital · Director of Strategy · VP Marketing

**Company size:** 2–50 employees. Small enough that the person you find makes decisions.

**The strongest signal:** their website sells AI visibility as a service but shows no numbers
behind it — no sample report, no explanation of how it is measured. That is the exact profile
we want to talk to.

### GROUP B — 15 names · people who have never heard of this category

Marketing leaders at brands selling **considered, higher-priced purchases** — furniture,
mattresses, cosmetic and aesthetic practices, dental, home services, medical devices, B2B
software over roughly $5k.

**Titles:** Marketing Director · VP Marketing · CMO · Head of Growth · Owner (for practices)

**The signal here is absence.** Their profile and company site say nothing about AI, ChatGPT,
or LLMs. **That is what qualifies them.** A sample made only of people already excited about
AI measures excitement, not demand — so this group is not optional and is not filler.

## 5. EXCLUSIONS — SKIP THESE, NO EXCEPTIONS

- **Credible PR, and anyone who works there.** They contacted us first, which disqualifies
  them from this study for a technical reason: people who approach you have already shown
  they are interested, so counting them makes the result look better than it is.
- **Anyone Rehan or Ahmad knows personally.** A friend agreeing to help produces a polite
  conversation and no information. It is the most expensive kind of wrong answer available.
- Anyone who has already been contacted about aivis.
- Recruiters, coaches, "AI thought leaders," course sellers, anyone whose main output is
  LinkedIn posts about AI.
- Enormous agencies (200+ staff) — the person you can reach there does not decide anything.

## 8. WHY 40 AND NOT 5

We need **five completed conversations.** Cold outreach to strangers typically converts in
the low tens of percent, so 40 names is the realistic cost of five conversations. If the
response rate turns out much higher or much lower, that is itself worth knowing and gets
recorded.

Do not stop early because the first few replies come quickly. Do not pad the list with easy
names to reach 40 faster — 25 correctly-filtered names beat 40 loose ones, and if the search
runs dry, say so rather than loosening §3.
